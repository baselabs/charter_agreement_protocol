[CAP] Charter Agreement Protocol

Agreements as evidence,
including when the parties are agents.

CAP is an open format and verification protocol for agreements between two parties. Its artifacts are byte-exact: the terms are unsigned canonical JSON, and the other four artifacts are signed compact JWS. From them anyone can check what the two parties agreed, which revision governed a given instant, and whether a signed action named the revision that governed when it happened, with no central service and without CAP making the call for you. CAP verifies. It never authorizes.

The five artifacts

Each one is a set of bytes you can hand to anyone, and anyone can verify it from those bytes alone.

ArtifactFormCarries
Party Descriptorsigned JWS (cap+party)a party’s key history (Ed25519 keys, and ML-DSA keys from protocol revision 3); each change is signed by the previous active key, and a branch in the history is kept as fork evidence
Charter Revisionunsigned canonical JSONthe proposed terms, which become agreed when both parties accept them: the two parties and their roles, the legal-text digest, precedence, the effective window, the termination reasons and exact ABP deployment bindings
Acceptancesigned JWS (cap+acceptance)one party’s signed assent to the exact revision bytes; a revision is accepted when both parties have signed one
Termination Noticesigned JWS (cap+termination)a signed notice that closes the charter from its effective instant, when it names the revision governing at that instant
Receiptsigned JWS (cap+receipt)the issuing party’s signed claim about one action, bound to exact revision coordinates, the deployment digest and a grant reference (a bap reference carries its digest; a host reference may omit it)

What it proves, and what it leaves to you

CAP returns facts about the bytes and a fixed list of things it did not check. Your application reads the facts and makes every business call itself.

Use CAP for

  • agreement evidence that two independent parties exchange as bytes and either can re-verify
  • agent commerce, where one or both parties are software agents
  • supplier terms, with proof of which revision governed an action
  • key-history continuity, including post-quantum key rotation
  • action receipts that anyone can re-verify offline

Do not use CAP for

  • deciding whether an action is allowed: CAP never authorizes
  • live revocation checks
  • evaluating terms: CAP reports term_satisfaction as not verified
  • legal adjudication
  • single-party self-attestation

Why the evidence holds

Byte-exact by construction.

Two systems each hold a “version 2”, and nobody can settle the dispute from the bytes.

Strict unpadded base64url, deterministic tagged decoding, RFC 8785 canonicalization and domain-separated SHA-256. An acceptance binds the exact revision bytes.

Contested views stay contested.

A tool that quietly picks one of two signed branches hides the dispute it found.

When one signer signs two branches, CAP keeps both as signed evidence and picks no winner. A new revision accepted by both parties repairs the fork when it descends from or supersedes every competing branch.

Two implementations, no shared code.

One implementation checking itself says little about the specification.

A 163-case conformance corpus runs through the Elixir verifier and an independent, builtins-only TypeScript verifier. A CAP release requires byte-identical reports from both.
